00What changed in v2.1

You asked for two things: real research on every tool instead of quick picks, and a design for how departments talk to each other. Seven research agents produced sourced surveys, one per domain, with every claim tagged as verified against a primary source, reported from a secondary source, or unknown. This revision is built on those. The five findings that changed the design:

  • Amazon now regulates agents contractually. Section 19 of the Business Solutions Agreement, effective March 4, 2026, requires automated tools to identify themselves in every request, log every action for 12 months, keep human authorization for large actions, and use registered API apps only. Browser automation and scraping are banned. The tier system was already stricter than this; the write path now satisfies it by construction.
  • DataDoe can write, not just read. Its MCP supports listing and price updates and full Amazon Ads campaign changes, each action type off by default, with dry run and an audit log. It stays the read layer and the interactive exploration tool. For unattended writes the design uses a private SP-API app you own instead, for reasons in section 07.
  • Claude Code Routines run with no approval prompts. That rules them out for any department that can write to the account. Unattended departments run on Claude Managed Agents, which has per-session dollar budgets, credential vaults, persistent memory, a coordinator for meetings, and a session viewer. That is also the monitoring you wanted.
  • Free first-party data replaces most paid tools. Brand Analytics search reports, the Product Pricing API's expected Buy Box price, listing issue and offer change notifications, and Amazon's own automated pricing rules cover what keyword, competitor, and repricing tools sell. The paid list shrinks to DataDoe, Keepa, one keyword tool during launches, and accounting.
  • The US launch has a compliance calendar, not just a stock calendar. FDA food facility registration renews only in October to December 2026. FSVP, prior notice, and Grocery category requirements are real workstreams. Two of those items could not be fully verified and are listed as open.

01Why v1 failed, and the one rule for v2

Version 1 was a software project. Python daemons on Hetzner, a custom sync layer, Mem0, a Telegram bot, a dashboard roadmap. It produced infrastructure, not decisions, and you never used it. The loop that actually ran the business was you, a Claude session, and live account data through DataDoe's MCP.

Version 2 takes that loop and gives it structure, a schedule, memory, and authority to act. It adds zero servers.

The rule: every component is either a hosted tool we buy, or a plain text file in a git repo. If something needs deploying, restarting, or debugging at 2am, it does not belong.

Three ideas survive from v1: humans approve money, business rules live in one constitution file, and knowledge compounds from observations into playbooks.

03Authority tiers and the trust ratchet

Tier 0
Observe and report
Read data, write findings to the brief and the wiki. No account writes. Every department starts here for its first week.
Tier 1
Act inside guardrails, logged
Reversible, bounded actions without asking. Today that is Advertising only, through Amazon's own Ads MCP: bids within ±15%, negatives past a statistical threshold, budgets within +25% per action up to a daily cap, one change per target per 24 hours. Money never moves on Tier 1; a bid change spends ad budget you already set.
Tier 2
Propose, Rami approves
Price changes, purchase orders, new campaigns, listing text or images, FBA shipment creation, coupons and deals, buyer messages, Vine enrolment, reimbursement claims. Proposals arrive in the morning brief as a numbered list. You answer in one message in your session, and the session executes.
Tier 3
Rami only
New marketplaces, brand or supplier contracts, payment terms, spend above the monthly PO ceiling, appeals and plans of action to Amazon, IP complaint responses, anything legal or regulatory. Agents prepare the packet; the decision and the submission are yours.

The ratchet. An action class moves from Tier 2 to Tier 1 when 30 days have passed, you approved at least 20 proposals of that class, and you rejected fewer than 5%. The Chief of Staff proposes, you confirm. Any class demotes by editing one line in the constitution. Amazon's own floor is human authorization for price moves over 20% in 24 hours and bulk edits of 500 or more ASINs; every tier here is stricter than that, so the ratchet can never drift below the policy.

04How departments communicate

You were right that v2 had no inter-department communication, and that this would cap quality. Supply Chain cannot size a purchase order without Finance's cash forecast and Advertising's launch plan. Pricing cannot cut a price while Advertising is buying rank on that SKU. The fix is not agents chatting freely. Free-form conversation between agents is expensive, unauditable, and drifts. The design uses three channels with increasing cost, and Anthropic's own guidance for multi-agent systems is the same: share state for independent work, hand off typed requests, and coordinate only when the decision genuinely crosses boundaries.

Shared statealways on · the blackboard

Every department writes to a fixed file others must read before proposing anything. Refreshed each run.

  • state/inventory.md cover per SKU, inbound ETAs, capacity
  • state/cash.md position, 8-week forecast, PO ceiling left
  • state/ads.md launches in ramp, pacing, ACOS by SKU
  • state/prices.md price, floor, competitor positions, tests
  • state/catalog.md changes in flight, suppressed listings
  • state/health.md account status, open violations
  • state/calendar.md launches, deals, blackout dates
Typed requestsasynchronous · the inbox

One file per request in requests/<dept>/inbox/ with a fixed schema and an enumerated type. Each department answers its inbox before its own work. Unanswered past the needed-by date escalates to the Chief of Staff.

  • need-cash-check Supply Chain → Finance
  • need-forecast Advertising → Supply Chain
  • need-margin-floor Pricing → Finance
  • blackout Advertising → Pricing: hold price on SKU until date
  • stockout-risk Supply Chain → Advertising: throttle spend
  • competitor-oos Pricing → Advertising: raise bids
  • quality-issue Customer → Supply Chain, Catalog
Meetingssynchronous · coordinated

One Managed Agents session where the Chief of Staff is the coordinator and departments are roster threads. The coordinator poses the decision, collects positions, resolves against the constitution, writes minutes to meetings/, and queues anything Tier 2 or above for you.

  • Monday business review, all departments
  • Monthly sales and operations planning: demand → supply → cash → launch calendar. POs are sized here.
  • Launch review for every new SKU or market
  • Event meetings: hero SKU stockout imminent, competitor out of stock, account health drop

Why this maps onto Managed Agents exactly

Managed Agents multi-agent sessions give a coordinator a roster of up to 20 agents, each in its own persistent thread with its own model, prompt, tools, and MCP servers. Threads share one filesystem, which is the repo. The coordinator sends tasks and follow-ups and receives reports as typed thread messages. There is no peer-to-peer channel and delegation is one level deep. That is not a limitation for this company; it is the audit trail. Every cross-department exchange either goes through files in git or through the Chief of Staff, and the session viewer shows every thread. An advisor entry lets the Chief of Staff consult a stronger model on a hard call mid-meeting. A session budget caps what a meeting can spend.

What you see

  • The morning brief lists unresolved cross-department conflicts as decisions, with each department's position in two lines.
  • Every meeting produces one page of minutes. You can join any meeting live from your session or read the minutes later.
  • The Chief of Staff rejects proposals that ignore relevant state, for example a price cut during a ranking push, and says why in the brief.

05Runtime and monitoring

Two surfaces, one repo, one set of connectors.

Unattended departmentsClaude Managed Agents
Each department is a stored, versioned agent. Scheduled deployments fire sessions on a cron in Asia/Jerusalem time. The company repo is attached as a GitHub resource; skills load from .claude/skills. Credentials live in a vault and are substituted at egress, never visible in the sandbox. Each deployment carries a hard dollar budget. Memory stores hold per-department memory across sessions. Meetings are multi-agent sessions with the Chief of Staff as coordinator. Pricing is standard tokens plus $0.08 per active session hour; idle is free. Unattended sessions execute Tier 1 only; Tier 2 proposals are written to the queue.
Your cockpitClaude Code, web or desktop
Your interactive sessions open the same repo with the same MCP connectors. You read the brief, answer the decision queue in one message, and the session executes the approved actions through the same tools and writes the same ledger entries. Deep work happens here: "show me why Advertising wants to cut the tahini campaign" opens the ledger entry and the data behind it. Runs on Claude Max.
Monitoringbuilt in
The Managed Agents console shows every session, thread, tool call, and cost. Deployment runs record every firing, including failures, so a department that did not run is visible. Webhooks can notify on session state changes. The brief itself is the daily health check: the Chief of Staff reports which departments ran and which did not. This is the monitoring you asked Grok for; it exists here already.
Where Routines fitClaude Code, optional
Routines are scheduled Claude Code cloud sessions on your subscription, but they run with no permission prompts and every connector attached by default. Use them only for read-only jobs, such as a Sunday ops digest, never for a department that can write.

Kill switch. Revoke the vault credentials and pause deployments. Both are one action in the console. Amazon's Agent Policy requires that agents stop on request; this is how.

06The write path and Amazon's Agent Policy

Amazon added Section 19, the Agent Policy, to the Business Solutions Agreement effective March 4, 2026, with a grace period that ended in early June. As reported across seller law firms and analysts, it requires:

  • Agents identify as automated in every request, with Agent/[name] in the User-Agent header, and never mimic humans or bypass CAPTCHAs.
  • All automated actions flow through registered SP-API applications. Browser automation, scraping, and undocumented endpoints are prohibited.
  • Every automated action is logged with timestamp, type, inputs, and outputs, retained at least 12 months.
  • A real human authorizes bulk edits of 500 or more ASINs, price changes above 20% in 24 hours, and account configuration changes.
  • No pricing decisions driven by scraped competitor data. Agents cease access when Amazon asks.

The policy text itself was not fetchable from this environment; the obligations above are consistent across six independent summaries and should be confirmed against the live agreement before go-live.

Why not DataDoe for unattended writes

DataDoe's writes are genuinely well designed: off by default per action type, dry run, audit history, and the company reports SOC 2 Type II and a cleared Amazon Data Protection Policy assessment. It is the most credible hosted write path found. Three reasons it is the read layer and not the unattended write path:

  • It cannot create FBA inbound shipments, submit feeds, or file reimbursement cases, so a second write path would be needed anyway.
  • Writes would run under DataDoe's SP-API app identity, not yours. The Agent Policy's identification and logging obligations are cleaner when the app is yours.
  • The audit trail would live in DataDoe rather than in your ledger. The ledger in git is the 12-month log the policy asks for and the one thing every department reads.

DataDoe writes are still useful in your interactive session for a one-off price or listing edit while the private app is being registered in week one. Enable only the listing action type, keep dry run on, and record the action in the ledger by hand.

07DataDoe, fully

DataDoe is built by Deltologic, a Polish marketplace technology agency of about 30 engineers founded in 2020. It began as a chat copilot in 2025 and repositioned in 2026 as an "Amazon data and action layer for AI". Its public GitHub repository is a schema stub for MCP registries, not the server, so the product is closed and hosted only. The tool contract in that stub is genuine, and it is the source for the capability list below. DataDoe's own website was blocked from this environment, so pricing and features from its pages are reported from search excerpts and marked accordingly.

What it connects

  • Verified: Amazon Seller Central via SP-API, Vendor Central, Amazon Ads across Sponsored Products, Brands, Display, TV, and DSP, and Brand Analytics including Search Query Performance, market basket, repeat purchase, and demographics. Marketplaces include Canada explicitly. Multiple accounts and marketplaces in one organisation.
  • Reported only: Walmart, Shopify, and TikTok Shop appear on marketing pages but not in the MCP tool contract. Treat them as dashboard-only or roadmap until tested in your account.

  • Continuous sync on most feeds into DataDoe's warehouse; queries return pre-synced data in under a second. No independent measurement of lag was found.   • Standard plan is a flat $97 per month with a 14-day trial, 2,000 write-action tokens, 10 million rows, one seat. Enterprise is custom with no rate limits. Overage rules are not published.
  • Claims SOC 2 Type II and a cleared Amazon Data Protection Policy assessment, and "Amazon-audited" developer status. No independent reviews exist anywhere; the only comparisons are DataDoe's own.

Playbook encoded into the charter

  • Four campaigns per hero SKU: discovery (auto plus broad), phrase testing, exact for rank acquisition, brand defence. Product targeting for conquesting once margins allow.
  • Branded and non-branded targets never share a campaign. Rejected structurally, not advised.
  • Negatives only above a click and spend threshold derived from the trailing 90-day conversion rate. No dayparting or rule automation on a campaign with under 10 days of history.
  • Target ACOS per SKU from contribution margin, never one account-wide number. Starting budget split roughly 20–25% discovery, 25–30% phrase, 45–55% exact and defence.
  • Guardrails: bid ceiling and floor per SKU tier; budget moves capped at +25% per action; one change per target per 24 hours; a daily spend cap enforced in the skill, not just the prompt.

Your idea was that Grok could run the always-on, communicating agents with monitoring, and Claude would be for your working sessions.
